The Museum Companion

Logistics and lesson planning unified for educators.

Role Product Designer
Timeline 5 Weeks

Product Overview

I designed the Museum Planning Companion to help teachers handle field trip logistics and lesson planning in one unified tool. By organizing chaperone ratios, student tracking, and accessibility maps into a single dashboard, it handles the messy admin tasks so teachers can focus on guiding their students.

Primary KPI

Increase Conversions

Increase the conversion rate of school group bookings by reducing drop-offs during the complex logistics phase.

Secondary KPI

Reduce Support Load

Decrease the volume of manual customer support emails/calls to museum staff by automating chaperone ratio math and centralizing accessibility PDFs.

Research & Discovery

I combined qualitative interviews with competitive reviews and quantitative surveys to map out the planning challenges from different angles.

Insight 1

Ratio Calculations

70% of teachers stated calculating chaperone ratios was the single most time-consuming part of trip prep.

Insight 2

Accessibility Fragmentation

Teachers with students needing accommodations had no single source of truth, piecing together info from emails, PDFs, and calls.

Insight 3

Mobile-First Realities

Most planning happens on a phone between classes, not at a desk. A desktop-only tool was an immediate non-starter.

Research Methodology & Affinity Mapping

I conducted 5 remote interviews (30–40 min) with elementary school educators and surveyed 10 more to quantify manual logistics vs. lesson planning. I clustered the feedback into themes like Logistics Anxiety and Accessibility Roadblocks to directly shape our primary persona, Tara (The Group Planner).

Competitive Review

I audited two direct competitors (ROM, AGO) and one indirect competitor (Tiqets). The audit revealed massive gaps between authoritative institutional content and modern, mobile-first logistics.

Gap 1

Manual Booking

Museum sites have authority but rely on slow, manual email booking for groups.

Gap 2

Individual Focus

Ticketing apps are fast but designed for individuals, ignoring group chaperone math.

Gap 3

Static Accessibility

Crucial accessibility info is hidden in static PDFs rather than built into the trip plan.

Target User: Tara, The Group Planner

Primary Goal: Logistical Clarity. Top Feature Needed: Group Checklist. Tara is a planned professional whose success metric is getting a booking approved by the school board without revisions. Secondary personas included Marcus (focused on predictability and safety) and Raf (focused on speed discovery).

Ideation & User Flow

I mapped out Tara's journey to see exactly where she hits roadblocks, exploring several concepts before moving to high-fidelity wireframes.

Rejected

Standalone Calculator

A simple tool focused exclusively on solving the chaperone-to-student math. Trade-off: Solved one problem but left accessibility planning as a separate, disconnected step.

Rejected

Chat-Based Assistant

An interactive chat interface to guide teachers through booking steps. Trade-off: Felt tedious for busy teachers who wanted to scan all details at a glance.

Selected

Unified Dashboard

A single overview hub displaying trip status, ratio calculations, and accessibility options together, matching how teachers actually think about a trip.

Refining the Dashboard

Once I had a working MVP, I tested it with real data. Testing with longer trip names and dense entries broke the layout, leading to four distinct refinement phases to clean up the UI.

V1 (First Pass)

Text was getting cut off and clipped when handling longer school trip names.

V2 (Fix 1)

Adjusted the CSS to allow text wrapping instead of harsh clipping.

V3 (Fix 2)

Switched to a clearer linear timeline layout to show the progression of tasks.

Final Polish

Filtered out duplicate/irrelevant data so the dashboard only shows what's actionable.

View Refined Prototype

Validation & Accessibility

I tested the prototype against a real scenario: logging in, checking trip status, calculating the ratio for 25 students, and exporting an accessible route map.

Testing Insight

Actionable Cards

Teachers requested a way to act on unreturned slips directly. I added a "Send Reminder Ping" toggle inside the pending trip card to close the loop faster.

Testing Insight

Dynamic Defaults Prevent Errors

Building standard school board ratios directly into the inputs as automatic defaults eliminated the ratio-calculation mistakes observed in earlier rounds.

Accessibility & Design System Highlights

Interactive Accommodation: Wheelchair-accessible route toggles are built directly into the map screen. High-Contrast Aesthetics: The UI utilizes a calm matcha green and dark charcoal palette to strictly meet WCAG AA text contrast standards.

Takeaways & Next Steps

Working on this pushed me to think about accessibility as a default. Building the "Dynamic Defaults" system meant accessibility needs had to be part of the data model from day one.

Step 1

Broad Usability Validation

Test the dashboard with a larger group of educators (10+) to see if the ratio flow holds up across different school sizes.

Step 2

Co-chaperone Interface

Explore a lightweight version for parent volunteers, who currently have no view into the plan.

Step 3

Reducing Admin Overhead

Validate with museum-side ticketing staff whether pre-validated submissions actually reduce their manual review cycles.
